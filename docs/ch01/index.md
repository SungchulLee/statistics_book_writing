# Data Collection

This chapter explores how data is collected and how we learn from it, contrasting the classical design-first approach with the modern data-first approach and introducing the three major machine learning paradigms.

!!! warning "Incomplete page"
    This is a chapter landing page and does not follow the five-section structure.

## Chapter Structure

### 1.1 Classical Approach

- **Populations and Samples** -- The fundamental distinction underlying all inference.
- **Parameters vs Statistics** -- Fixed unknowns versus computable summaries.
- **Observational Studies** -- Cross-sectional, cohort, case-control, and ecological designs.
- **Confounding and Association vs Causation** -- Why correlation does not imply causation.
- **Controlled Experiments** -- Treatment/control groups and random assignment for causal claims.
- **Randomization and Blinding** -- Distributing confounders and preventing expectation bias.
- **Surveys and Sampling Methods** -- SRS, stratified, cluster, and systematic sampling.
- **Bias and Nonresponse** -- Sampling, nonresponse, response, and selection bias.
- **Survivorship Bias** -- Analyzing survivors while ignoring failures.

### 1.2 Modern Approach

- **Statistical Models vs Learning Algorithms** -- Parametric models versus flexible data-driven methods.
- **Prediction vs Inference** -- Forecasting outcomes versus understanding relationships.

### 1.3 Three Learning Paradigms

- **Supervised Learning** -- Learning from labeled input-output pairs.
- **Unsupervised Learning** -- Discovering structure without labels.
- **Reinforcement Learning** -- Sequential decision-making via rewards.
